# CAISO transmission planning: historical reference

*Working reference. Updated September 10, 2026.*

## Summary

CAISO’s transmission planning process has developed from a coordinated set of utility and CAISO studies into an annual, comprehensive planning process. Its January 2007 Board memorandum identifies the jointly prepared 2006 CAISO Transmission Plan as the first of future annual plans.[^2006memo]

The formative period from 1998 through 2006 was not static. It began with a reliability- and transmission-owner-centered arrangement, developed through CAISO’s early planning-process reforms, and culminated in the jointly prepared 2006 annual plan. In 2010–12, CAISO revised and consolidated the process around reliability, public-policy, and economic needs.[^2010plan] Beginning with 2012–13, CAISO’s public library is organized as a continuous series of annual planning-cycle collections.[^library] Since 2022, the annual process has been more closely synchronized with CEC demand forecasts and CPUC resource planning and has been supplemented by CAISO’s 20-Year Transmission Outlook.[^cec]

## Detailed timeline

### 1998–2001: initial ISO-era planning

In the early ISO period, planning was not housed in one recurring, CAISO-authored plan package. The three major participating transmission owners—PG&E, SCE, and SDG&E—each prepared a transmission expansion plan. CAISO also performed a control-area-wide assessment and five-year expansion plan. The CPUC described this as a four-part *Grid Coordinated Planning Process*.[^cpuc2001]

Projects could also come forward through special studies or stakeholder processes, especially where a specific reliability or development question needed attention. The CPUC record describes the projects CAISO had approved since its inception as basic reliability projects, with costs rolled into utility rates.[^cpuc2001] This was a planning model focused primarily on maintaining system reliability rather than selecting broad categories of policy-driven transmission.

### 2001–2005: early process reform and stronger coordination

CAISO began developing what it called the “New ISO Transmission Planning Process” in 2001. The process was intended to make CAISO’s role more proactive and to improve coordination among CAISO, the transmission owners, and state agencies.[^2005filing] During this period, CAISO and the CPUC also worked on how CAISO determinations of transmission need would relate to the CPUC’s permitting process, particularly for economic transmission projects.[^2005filing]

The underlying planning structure still remained distributed. Transmission owners continued to develop their own plans, and project-specific studies and CPUC proceedings remained important parts of the record. The main change was the effort to give CAISO a more defined, system-wide planning role and to make the process more transparent and coordinated.

### 2006–2009: the first CAISO annual-plan format

CAISO presented the 2006 Transmission Plan to its Board in January 2007. The accompanying memorandum says the plan was jointly prepared by CAISO and the participating transmission owners, covered projects across several planning horizons, and was intended to be the first of future annual plans.[^2006memo]

This was still a period of process-building. CAISO’s 2007–11 business plan called for a California Sub-Regional Planning Group, a planning business-practice manual, tariff changes, congestion studies, and work to integrate renewable generation.[^businessplan] The federal backdrop also changed: FERC Order No. 890 established planning-process requirements emphasizing transparency, coordination, and stakeholder participation.[^order890]

The record for these years is real but uneven. A plan, a Board presentation, a business-practice manual, a PTO filing, and a CPUC case may all describe different pieces of the same planning work.

### 2010–2012: revised process and transition to the modern TPP

CAISO proposed revisions to its planning process in June 2010; the changes became effective December 20, 2010. The 2010–11 cycle began under the earlier process and concluded under the revised one. CAISO describes that plan as the first comprehensive plan taken to the Board under the revised process.[^2010plan]

The revised process organized planning around three questions:

1. **Reliability:** What facilities are needed to meet planning and reliability standards?
2. **Public policy:** What facilities are needed to support state or federal policy requirements, including renewable-energy targets?
3. **Economic efficiency:** Would transmission reduce congestion, production, loss, or other electric-supply costs?

It also documented non-transmission alternatives and request-window proposals more consistently. This is the point at which the annual plan begins to look much more like the process familiar today.

FERC Order No. 1000, issued in 2011, reinforced the regional framework: it required regional planning, consideration of policy-driven needs, regional and interregional cost-allocation methods, and removal of the federal right of first refusal for certain regional facilities.[^order1000]

### 2012–13 onward: stable annual-cycle archive

CAISO’s current library has an individual collection for every cycle beginning with 2012–13.[^library] This is the best starting point for any plan-level history or dataset because it usually preserves the plan, appendices, studies, drafts, Board materials, and—in applicable cycles—competitive-solicitation information.

The mature process retains the three-part reliability / public-policy / economic framework. Current process materials show the recurring sequence: study assumptions, technical assessments, stakeholder meetings, a request window for alternatives, a draft plan, and Board approval. Some approved regional projects then move to competitive solicitation.[^tppoverview]

### 2022 onward: closer alignment with state resource planning and a longer horizon

In 2022, the CEC, CPUC, and CAISO adopted a memorandum of understanding to better align demand forecasting, resource planning and procurement, interconnection, and transmission planning.[^cec] That same year CAISO published its first 20-Year Transmission Outlook; an update followed in 2024.[^cec]

The outlook is a long-range planning tool. It should not be treated as the same thing as an annual TPP approval. For a project history, keep outlook scenarios, annual-plan recommendations, developer selection, permitting, and construction as separate milestones.

## How the record is organized

The form of the available documentation reflects the way the planning process was organized at the time.

| Period | Main sources | Character of the record |
|---|---|---|
| 1998–2001 | Individual PTO expansion plans; CAISO control-area assessments; CAISO Board materials; CPUC decisions | Reliability- and project-centered. The record is distributed across the PTO plans and CAISO’s system-wide assessment rather than collected in an annual CAISO plan package. |
| 2001–05 | CAISO planning-process materials; PTO plans; CAISO Board materials and special studies; CPUC proceedings on transmission need and permitting | Reform and coordination period. The sources document CAISO’s expanding system-wide role, but planning remains distributed among several institutions and proceedings. |
| 2006–09 | CAISO annual-plan memoranda and presentations; Transmission Planning BPM; PTO plans; Board materials; CPUC/CEC/RETI materials | Early annual-plan period. The CAISO plan provides a useful anchor, but the supporting record is still less standardized than in later annual cycles. |
| 2010–12 | 2010–11 and 2011–12 plans, appendices, stakeholder materials, and FERC/CAISO process filings | Transitional. The original document labels and terminology show how the new process was being put into place. |
| 2012–13 onward | CAISO *Transmission plans and studies* library | Annual-cycle-based. The final plan, appendices, study assumptions, drafts, and Board decision are generally available together. |
| 2022 onward | Annual TPP materials plus CEC/CPUC planning inputs and the 20-Year Outlook | The annual plan remains the vehicle for project approvals; the Outlook provides a longer-range planning context. |

### Reading a project through the planning process

A transmission project can appear at several distinct points in the public record: as a study need, a proposed solution, an approved transmission-plan element, a competitively selected or assigned project, a permitted project, and an in-service facility. These are separate milestones. CAISO’s annual planning process identifies system needs and functional solutions; detailed routing, environmental review, construction authorization, and implementation occur later through transmission owners and the relevant permitting authorities.[^cec]

Planning-cycle labels also differ from approval dates. A cycle named “2012–2013,” for example, generally concludes in the second year.
